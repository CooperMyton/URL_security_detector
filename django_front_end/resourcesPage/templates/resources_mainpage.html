This is turning out to be more like just programming in html and css, at least for the frontend, but it is what it is. A URL is the address of a website, kind of like a home address for the internet. When you click a link, your browser trusts that address and takes you wherever it leads. Most of the time this is harmless, but sometimes URLs are designed to trick people. A bad URL might look like a real website you know—such as your school, bank, or email provider—but it can secretly lead to a fake page made to steal passwords or personal information. This is why URL security matters, especially for people who don’t spend much time thinking about links before clicking them. Attackers often create risky URLs by copying trusted names, misspelling popular websites, adding extra words like “secure” or “login,” or hiding the real destination behind shortened links. These small changes are easy to miss, but they can make a big difference. Before clicking a link, it helps to slow down and check whether the address matches what you expect, looks correctly spelled, and starts with “https://.” If a link asks for personal information unexpectedly or feels slightly off, that’s usually a warning sign. The good news is that avoiding bad URLs doesn’t require advanced technical knowledge. Simple habits like not clicking on unexpected links, typing important website addresses yourself, and using tools to check URLs before trusting them can go a long way. URLs may look like random text, but they control where you go online. Learning how to recognize safe and unsafe URLs is one of the easiest ways to protect yourself on the internet.
